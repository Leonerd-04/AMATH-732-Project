Below, I explain the different plots. Feel free to ask me if something does not make sense...

[I] Spatial snapshots: The spatial snapshots show how positive and negative opinions spread throughout the two interacting circles over time. 
Each panel represents a different simulation timestep, with red indicating positive opinions, 
blue indicating negative opinions, and black representing uninfected individuals. 
The continuous colour scale also illustrates the strength of each individual's opinion, 
allowing changes in both spatial distribution and opinion intensity to be observed.

[II] Distribution of opinions: The histograms show the distribution of opinion strengths at five different points in the simulation. 
They illustrate how the balance between positive and negative opinions changes over time, 
as well as whether opinions tend to become stronger, weaker or more concentrated around particular values.

[III] Phase portrait: The phase portrait tracks the number of positive, N+, and negative, N−, 
individuals throughout the simulation. Each point represents the population state at a particular timestep, 
with the trajectory showing how the balance between the two competing opinions evolves. 
The green and red markers indicate the initial and final population states, respectively.

[IV] Echo-silo diagnostics segregation time series: This figure examines whether individuals "preferentially interact" 
with others who share their opinion. The first panel (k-NN segregation) measures the proportion of an individual's eight 
nearest neighbours who hold the same opinion. Higher values indicate stronger clustering of like-minded individuals, 
while the dotted lines represent the expected proportions if neighbours were distributed randomly according to the 
overall opinion balance. The second panel (segregation excess) measures the difference between observed (local) and 
expected same-opinion neighbour fractions (global). Positive values indicate segregation, while negative values indicate 
greater interaction with opposing opinions than would be expected randomly. The third panel (normalised opinion interface) 
measures the proportion of nearby pairs that hold opposing opinions. Higher values indicate more contact between competing opinion groups, 
whereas lower values suggest greater separation.

[V]Angular segregation: This figure examines how positive and negative opinions are distributed around each circle over time. 
The heatmaps divide each circle into 36 angular segments and show the majority opinion within each segment: 
red represents a positive majority, blue a negative majority, and grey indicates segments without a clear majority. 
This reveals how opinions cluster spatially and whether distinct regions of opposing opinions develop. 
The accompanying boundary plots count the number of transitions between neighbouring occupied angular segments with opposing majorities. 
A higher number of boundaries indicates more frequent changes between positive- and negative-dominated regions, 
while a lower number suggests larger, more consolidated regions of similar opinion. 
Together, these plots show how spatial opinion segregation develops independently within each circle.
